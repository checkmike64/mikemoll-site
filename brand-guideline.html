I help experts get paid for the value they create.

Service professionals who know they’re excellent at what they do — but feel like their business hasn’t caught up yet — work with Mike Moll to close that gap, without overcomplicating everything.

Locked copy. Use the headline word for word on the site hero, the LinkedIn headline and the first slide of every workshop. The sub-header sits under it on the site.

How we talk about the offer

90% of the problems service founders face are due to the offer. It’s usually one of four problems:

  1. 1Positioning that doesn’t attract ideal clients
  2. 2No unique factor to stand out in the AI era
  3. 3Delivery that’s bloated and hard to scale
  4. 4Pricing set to win contracts, with no room to grow

Mike finds the one holding a business back most and fixes it first, then moves to the next. Decided September 24, 2026.

Rules for the offer message

  • Mike’s title is Sales + Offer Coach. Pair it with “Podcast Host” where a second role fits.
  • Pricing is one of the four problems. Don’t lead with underpricing on its own, because every business coach does.
  • Say the problems “are due to the offer”. “Start with the offer” reads weaker.
  • Name the problem and the fix before the method. The Compression Method comes after the reader feels understood.

Who it’s for

Service-based founders who are struggling with their offer: consultants, coaches, agency owners and fractional experts. They’re great at their work and their clients get results, but the offer is holding the business back.

No income bracket. Mike gets opportunities at every revenue level, so we never target a revenue range. Never write anything that turns away someone making over $20K a month.

They come asking for more leads or better sales. The fix is almost always the offer.

Not for people with no paying clients yet, anyone in a cash crisis, e-commerce or SaaS owners, pure creatives, or anyone hunting for one silver bullet.

Working language, September 24, 2026. Mike is finishing the offer and its unique pieces, so this section will sharpen.

What we promise

The Compression Method finds the one constraint holding the business back the most, fixes it fast, then moves to the next one.

Lead with sales and the offer. Clients mention sales results most often. Keep pricing as one of the four offer problems. Show the move from fear to confidence, and end on the confidence.

Every piece points at one feeling: the rules they’ve been following were never fixed. They get to decide the offer, the price, the clients and the process.

Logo and mark

The logo is Mike.Moll in Libre Franklin 800 with a blue period. In the site header, “Sales + Offer Coach” sits under it in small caps. The mark is a white M on a black circle, and it’s the site’s favicon.

The blue dot

The blue dot is the signature mark. It comes from the period in Mike.Moll. On the site it marks bullets, eyebrows and the current page in the menu.

An eyebrow is the small caps line above a headline. Use them rarely: five at most on a long page, one on a short page. Always ink with the blue dot, never blue text, and never a repeat of the headline under it.

Colours

  • White#FFFFFFThe page. About 70% of a long page.
  • Soft grey#F4F6F8Soft bands that set a section apart. About 10 to 15%.
  • Navy#0B1B27Navy bands, the footer and the hero card. About 15 to 20%. The site’s only dark colour.
  • Ink#0C1A24Headlines, bold text and the lines of a glyph.
  • Body text#3A4651Paragraphs and lists.
  • Muted#5F6B76Captions, labels and small print.
  • Hairline#E4E9EEThe 1px lines between things.
  • Blue#38B6FFButtons, dots and the one blue part of a glyph. A fill on white, never text.
  • Deep blue#0874B8Links and numerals on white. The only blue text on white.

Which blue

Bright blue is never text on white. At 2.3 to 1 contrast it’s too faint to read. On white, blue text is the deep blue #0874B8. The bright blue #38B6FF is for fills: buttons, dots and glyph accents. On navy, the bright blue works as text.

  • Links and text on whiteSee client results#0874B8
  • Buttons and fillsBook your free assessment#38B6FF
  • Text on navy$386Kbest client result#38B6FF

Blue marks action and money. A headline word only turns blue on navy, and only when it’s about money. Button text is near-black #04131D, never white.

One corner: 6px

6px

Buttons, cards, photos, form fields and the hero card all get the same 6px corner. No pills.

Round is only for small profile photos, the blue dot and the favicon. The podcast covers in the badge stack get 9px.

Buttons

Book your free assessmentFree assessment

Solid blue with near-black text, 16px, sentence case. The second choice is an ink outline. One solid button per screen.

No uppercase, letter-spaced buttons. No white text on blue.

Hairlines over boxes

  • Positioning
  • Unique factor
  • Delivery
  • Pricing

Separate things with 1px hairlines and white space. Build hierarchy with size and space. Never put a box inside a box.

When you need a card, there’s one style: white, 1px hairline, 6px corner, no shadow. The test for every page: it should read like a dossier.

Navy bands

Navy is the site’s dark colour. Bands run the full width of the page with square edges. Black pages and black sections are retired.

A navy band does one of five jobs: proof figures, the “what it does” grid, testimonials, the closing call to action, or the footer.

  • Three at most on a long page, plus the footer.
  • Never two in a row, and never behind a form.
  • Short pages get none.
  • The hero card is the only navy box, and it gets the 6px corner.

Glyphs

Every glyph is drawn for the brand. Thin, round ink lines with exactly one blue part, and nothing behind it: no circle, square or tile.

No clip-art: no check circles, X circles or stock line icons. A new glyph gets drawn in this style.

Bullets, the x and numerals

Bullets

Bullets are small blue dots. The small thin x is only for things being dismissed, like myths or “not a fit”. Numerals only when the order matters. Never ✓, + or square bullets.

No long dashes

A dash or a bar is never decoration. That rules out dash bullets, short rules under headlines, accent bars on top of cards, and strike-throughs.

Underlines are for links only. To separate things, use a dot, a hairline or white space. In copy, no en or em dashes either (chapter 04).

Photos of Mike, in colour, at full strength. No ghosted or faded photos, no glows, no colour washes. Never alter Mike’s face.

What not to do on the site

  • Black pages or black sections. Use navy.
  • Bright blue #38B6FF as text on white. Use deep blue #0874B8.
  • A blue headline word that isn’t about money.
  • Pills, fully round buttons, or any corner but 6px.
  • Uppercase, letter-spaced buttons.
  • A box inside a box. Shadows, glows or gradient blobs.
  • Check marks, plus signs or square bullets.
  • Clip-art icons, or icons sitting in circles or squares.
  • Dash bullets, bars under headlines, accent bars on cards.
  • Ghosted, tinted or faded photos. Any change to Mike’s face.
  • An eyebrow on every section, or one that repeats its headline.
  • A second row of podcast logos under the badge stack.
  • A third typeface.

Social graphics

Carousels, Reel and TikTok covers, YouTube thumbnails.

Their own locks

Social graphics keep their own locked styles. The website update doesn’t change them. They share the two typefaces and the bright blue #38B6FF with the site, and these rules still stand:

  • Square corners everywhere. The M circle is the only exception.
  • White slides use #111111 ink. Dark slides use #F2F2F2 on #141414.
  • No gradients, gold or cream.
  • Icons are literal, drawn on one grid, with nothing behind them.
  • One accent per piece. Keep it off body text.
  • No third typeface.

Two exceptions, each locked in its builder: a cover may fade to black when the photo needs it, and a thumbnail puts a soft shadow under its words.

Voice and words

How Mike sounds, and the words we use and avoid.

  • Voice

Voice

Direct
The first sentence earns its place or it goes.
Peer-level
One business owner talking to another.
Specific
A named client and a figure. If a claim can’t be specific, cut it.
Calm
No hype, no urgency theater, no exclamation points.
Irreverent
When it fits. Name the absurd thing, lightly and accurately.

Fifth-grade reading level. Contractions. Active voice. One idea per sentence, in full sentences of mixed length. No em dashes.

Write“You’re charging $1,000 for something that puts $70,000 in your client’s pocket. The fix is a pricing skill, and you can learn it faster than you think.”

Skip“Many entrepreneurs struggle with pricing confidence and need to develop a stronger value mindset.”

Words

Use

owner-to-owner·the value your work creates·get paid for the value you create·compress, distill, simplify·business case·the skill was never the problem·simpler and more valuable·their industry’s best-kept secret·free 20-minute assessment

Retired in the July 2026 refresh

zone of genius·borrowed beliefs·the cage was never locked·on your terms·take it or leave it·worst-case math·snap-called·expansive·great at the work·what your work is worth

Retired in September 2026

solopreneur·income brackets as targeting (“stuck between $10K and $20K a month”)·“start with the offer” (say “are due to the offer”)·Pricing + Offer Coach

Never

mindset shift·clarity·empower·unlock your potential·journey·hustle·proven system·6- or 7-figure with no figure·leverage·deep dive·unpack·real·actually·my own math·$X worth of work·results you can point to·DM me

Also banned: “it’s not X, it’s Y” in any form, stacks of two-word sentences, and rhetorical-question openers. Where older docs disagree, the July 2026 refresh and the voice guardrails win.

Every piece, in this order

  1. 01Recognition. They see themselves in the description.
  2. 02Understanding. They feel understood about the offer and the price.
  3. 03Credibility. Mike’s story and client results show he’s been there.
  4. 04Method. Name The Compression Method, and only now.
  5. 05Offer. One next step.

No fear-based selling: name the situation without using burnout as a threat. No dependency messaging: the goal is the client’s independence.

20%Story and proof
  • The Corktown walk, the agency that nearly went under twice from adding too much, life on the road, podcast appearances. These show how Mike thinks. Keep them out of the headline topic.

Send hosts the speaker sheet link in the first email. Don’t attach a file. Ask Mike for access to the Notion database if it doesn’t open.

Before the first email

  1. 1Check the show fits. Published in the last 60 days, 20+ episodes, takes guests, has a website and social accounts, posts each episode with notes and links, and its listeners include service founders. Skip long pay-to-play application forms.
  2. 2Follow the host from Mike’s Instagram and LinkedIn. Paste both profiles into Host Instagram and Host LinkedIn in Notion, then tick Followed on Instagram and Followed on LinkedIn. On LinkedIn, press Follow. If there’s only Connect, connect with no note.
  3. 3For top-priority shows, show up first. Over the two weeks before the pitch, leave 2–3 specific comments on the host’s posts from Mike’s accounts. React to something they said. Never “Great post!”
  4. 4Find two recent episode titles close to Mike’s topics: offers, sales, pricing, positioning, delivery.
  5. 5Pick 2–3 episode ideas from the speaker sheet that fit this show’s listeners, and write one sentence on why they need the episode.
