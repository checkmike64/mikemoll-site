How to Market a Coaching Offer on Facebook

January 15, 202533 minLive Coaching
Listen on Spotify Apple Podcasts Watch on YouTube

Megan Van Noy coaches moms who run service businesses. Her brand is called Not Just a Mom. She sells a business bootcamp, a group mastermind, and one-on-one coaching. In this live coaching session she brought me one question. How do you market a coaching offer on Facebook without getting kicked out of the groups? Here is the short answer I gave her: give value first, ask for the sale as late as you can, and send warm people to an email list you own. The rest of this post walks through the plan.

Megan gets most of her customers from Facebook groups. Mom groups run hot. When members believe in something, they share it fast. That reach is a gift. The pitch is the risk. Post a hard sell in one of these groups and the admin boots you. So her reach was fine. She needed a way to show up that builds trust and still points people back to her offer.

My first piece of advice was to lead with the trainings she already has. Megan has practical videos that hit a mom right in the gut. Drop those into the groups with no call to action. People who want more will click her profile and find her offer on their own. I also told her to ask the group admin for permission first. Some will say no. Many say yes when you promise to teach and skip the pitch. A lot of coaches fear that giving away their content will kill sales. The opposite happens. People pay for content that is organized and put in order for them, even when the raw pieces sit out in the open.

Answer real questions with short Loom videos

The next move works inside any group. Search the threads for questions you can answer well. When you find one, record a short Loom video and walk through the fix. Say someone asks for feedback on a new consulting website. Record a nine-minute video, break down the problem, and post the link back in the thread. Loom lets you float a link over the video the entire time. That link can point to a free resource, a newsletter, or a short strategy call. After nine minutes of real help, people rarely mind the ask. This is the same value-first pattern that shows up when you market a live learning cohort: teach in public, then invite.

Warm people up before you run paid ads

Ads are coming for Megan, so we mapped the funnel. Do not open with the price. When you pick a target on Facebook, the pool can hold tens of millions of people. It takes time and money for them to see you once, let alone enough to trust you. So warm them up in stages. Run video that teaches. Then run testimonial ads that show one person going from stuck to solved. Then run the plain offer: click if you want this outcome, skip it if you don't. People buy when they feel you understand them, and a few touch points before the ask build that feeling. Knowing who you are targeting matters as much as the sequence, which is why I keep coming back to defining your ideal client and ignoring everyone else.

Send warm traffic to an email list you own

We also talked about where all this traffic should land. Megan has a lot of content on YouTube. YouTube is a soft landing place, but she does not own that audience. If the platform changes its rules, her reach drops overnight. An email list she owns for good. So point warm people to a light opt-in instead of a buy button. At the start of each YouTube video, ask for the subscribe and give one reason to hop over to the email list, like a piece of long-form work they can only get there. Email has stayed my biggest source of growth and revenue across every medium I have tried. If you want a deeper look at building around an owned list, this session on monetizing ideas for a free newsletter covers it.

Build a separate funnel for referrals

Megan sits on a goldmine most coaches never get. Every round of her program brings five to ten referrals from past clients. Her members named themselves Megan's Monsters and recruit on their own. Here is the gap. When a member tags a stranger in a group, that tag gives Megan credit, but the stranger still does not know her. Asking that person to pull out a credit card is a big first step. So I told her to build a separate email sequence for referral leads. Give them a case study or two, some FAQs, and answers to the doubt in their head before any money comes up. Make it easy for members to share by handing them a small library of your best assets, so the right link goes out instead of a cold homepage.

Key takeaways

  • Give value inside Facebook groups before you ask for anything, and ask the group admin for permission first.
  • Post your best trainings with no call to action. Warm people will find your offer on their own.
  • Answer real questions with short Loom videos and float a soft link over the video.
  • For paid ads, teach first, show a testimonial next, then make the plain offer.
  • Send warm traffic to an email list you own, not to a buy button on a platform you rent.
  • Give referral leads their own email sequence, since a tagged stranger still needs proof before they buy.
About the guest

Megan Van Noy

Megan Van Noy is the founder of Not Just a Mom, a brand and coaching program for moms who run service businesses. She started as a dental hygienist, specialized in myofunctional therapy, and built a six-figure global telehealth practice.
