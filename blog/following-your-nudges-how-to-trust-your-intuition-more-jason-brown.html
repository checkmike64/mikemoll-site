How to trust your intuition by following your nudges

Jason Brown of The Misfit Collective calls the small signals he gets nudges, and he builds his life and work around them. On this episode he explains how breathwork made them louder, why he starts each day in the feeling he wants, and the easy first steps for skeptics.

Short answer

How do you trust your intuition?

Start by noticing the small signals you already get, like an idea in the shower or a person who pops into your head, and act on one of them. Jason Brown calls these signals nudges. He says they got louder once breathwork quieted his thinking mind, and the more he followed them, the more things fell into place. Practices like breathwork, sound healing and meditation help, as long as you put the message to use in daily life.

What is a nudge?

A nudge is a small signal that tells you where to go, who to talk to or what to do next. Jason Brown says it can show up as a whisper when you wake up, an idea in the shower, or a person you think of who then calls you. Some cultures call these synchronicities or omens. Others call it luck. Jason calls them nudges.

I met Jason last summer through our friend Curran. We went on a bike ride and a walk, and he told me about following nudges. It sounded a little odd in the moment. The more I thought about it, the more it fit my own life.

Jason has followed nudges for years and has gone deep on them in the last two. He says the more inner work he did, the louder the nudges got. Now he can often feel the answer in his body when it's clear. Even our walk came from one. Curran texted him that he was out with me, and Jason dropped in.

How do you start to notice your intuition?

For Jason, it started with breathwork. He went to an hour-long session with a practitioner named Jen Mansell, and his body started moving with energy. At the same time, new ideas came in and he saw his problems in a new light.

The second time, at Curran's event, everyone wrote their downloads on Post-it notes. Jason still has his. One of them said "welcome back to myself." He saw that these whispers had been there his whole life. The answers he got that day were better than anything his thinking brain had come up with in weeks.

He didn't trust it fully at first. So he did what breathwork teachers suggest. He journaled the downloads and put them into practice, and things sped up. He'd think of someone during a session, then see them within minutes or hours, or they'd call him out of the blue. Each time, he got a bit more confident that this was more than chance.

Why doesn't hitting your goals feel better for longer?

The good feeling fades fast, and then you want more. Jason says that's the ego at work. It lives in the past or the future and wants to collect something it doesn't have yet. He compares it to winning a basketball championship. You party for a few nights, go to Vegas, have the parade, and then it's "Okay, now what?"

I know that loop. Years ago I had an app, and we went on a Dragon's Den spin-off show and got a deal. The moment it ended, my first thought was "Cool, what's next?" For stretches of my life I didn't want to celebrate anything, because I knew the high would come with a low on the back end.

Jason worked out that he'd been chasing a feeling the whole time, and the things he collected were just a way to get it. I ask the people I coach where it ends. The first 10 things they say they want usually aren't what they want.

The same idea, for your businessJoe Rando builds a one-person business around life goals first. Read the success cycle episode.

What does it mean to feel it first?

Feeling it first means you start your day in the feeling you want, like joy, freedom or love, before you have the thing you think will bring it. Jason noticed those feelings showed up during breathwork and meditation. So he didn't have to go and collect anything to feel them.

One of his coaches, Matthew Patty, put it this way: "Your energy precedes you." Jason runs his life the other way around now. Most of us say we'll believe it when we see it. He says that when he believes it, he sees it.

He uses memory to get there. Take a past win, like my Dragon's Den deal, go back to that memory and feel it now. Then start each day in that feeling and send it ahead of you. He says that when he lives from that feeling, the person he needs shows up and the joy comes much faster in his business. He calls it taking the rocket ship, and he compares the old way to a slog up the stairs.

How does breathwork help you hear your intuition?

Breathwork gets your thinking brain out of the way so other ideas can come in. Jason gives business people a picture for it. Imagine a board meeting with great board members. They take you out of the room and keep working to move your business forward. That's how breathwork felt to him. His busy thoughts got pushed aside, and wisdom came in.

When he followed what came through, things moved faster and lined up in ways he didn't expect. Now, whenever he feels stuck or feels friction, he goes back to the breath. He calls it the most accessible cheat code he's found.

He likes the longer style that Jen Mansell teaches, where you go into another state. He finds it powerful, very accessible and safe. He thinks the Wim Hof style is useful for your body, and he uses the longer kind to tap into wisdom.

What's an easy first step if you're skeptical?

Pick one simple practice and try it. Jason believes all bridges inward are valid. His favorites are breathwork and sound healing. Meditation, a Joe Dispenza training, listening to frequencies and ecstatic dance all count too.

He thought ecstatic dance was dumb at first. Everyone dances around, mostly with their eyes closed. Then he tried it and loved it. Once he let go of worrying about how he looked, he went into a different state.

Sound baths work for me. I pick three stories or places in my life I want to visit, and I watch them like a movie while the sound bath plays. If you're on a tight budget, you can find these on Insight Timer and listen for free.

Jason's own first step was plant medicine. He also knows how this sounds. For years he couldn't hear the people who talked about it, because it seemed too woo-woo to him. So his advice is to look at all the bridges and find yours.

Why does community matter for inner work?

Jason thinks getting into a community is the most important first step. People who are already doing the inner work can be the bridge for you. And once you go deeper, he says you're meant to turn around and light the way for the next person.

He's in a mastermind with Aubrey Marcus, and he suggests Aubrey's podcast as a good bridge. Many of the guests had big success first and then went inward.

Jason is also building a place for this. It's called First Place, and he describes it as something like Soho House for people who want to try these practices together. He points out that our cities are built around banks, telcos and insurance companies, and he wants a new kind of gathering place for inner work. He knows thousands of great people in Toronto, and he still asks where they'd go for a sound healing or an ecstatic dance.

How do you use what your intuition tells you?

Act on it in your everyday life. Jason has seen that many of the masterminds and spiritual groups he's visited don't do much with what they learn. He shared a line he's heard from teachers he follows: when you get the message, hang up the phone. The point of any practice is to take the message and use it.

If you don't apply what you learn, you keep running the same loops you've run for decades. Jason says he ran his for five decades. He was 50 when we recorded this.

He tried living fully on the spiritual side for a while. He wore the robes and grew the big beard, then came back because everyday life is where he feels most fulfilled. Now he coaches 13 CEOs who have wins on the board but find the good feeling doesn't last. He helps them change how they keep score.

What can you do this week?

  1. Write down any idea or person that pops into your head, and act on one of them.
  2. Try one longer breathwork session, and journal what comes up right after.
  3. Pick a past win, go back to that memory, and notice how it felt.
  4. Start each morning in that feeling, and watch what shows up.
  5. Try a free sound healing on Insight Timer, or go to an ecstatic dance.
  6. Find one group already doing inner work, and share what you're learning with them.
