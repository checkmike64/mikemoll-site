TLDR

  • You can't delegate what you have never written down.
  • Bucket your tasks by type, then hire against the bucket that hurts most.
  • There is no unicorn hire who does everything, so stop looking for one.

Mike joined Penny Zenker on Take Back Time to talk about the trap most founders fall into. They know they should delegate, but they micromanage anyway, and the business stays stuck on their shoulders.

Mike learned this the hard way running an agency for thirteen years. Here is the simple system he uses to get out from under the work.

Record the task before you hand it off

Most delegation fails before it starts because the process only lives in the founder's head. Mike's fix is low effort on purpose. Next time you do a task, hit record on a rough Loom and just talk through it as you go. It does not need to be pretty. Now the steps exist outside your brain, which means someone else can follow them. If you skip this, you will hover, correct, and take the task back, and you are right back to micromanaging.

Bucket your tasks by type

Once you have a pile of recorded tasks, group them by type. Admin in one bucket. Client delivery in another. Marketing in another. This does two things. It shows you where your time goes, and it stops you from writing a job posting that asks one person to be great at five unrelated things. That posting is how people end up chasing a hire who does not exist.

Hire against your biggest pain, not a unicorn

Here is the part Mike is firm about. There is no unicorn hire. Stop looking for the one person who will do everything and never make a mistake. Instead, look at your buckets and find the one causing you the most pain right now. Hire against that bucket first. When you delegate against a clear bucket with a recorded process, the handoff sticks, and you free up your time. Then you do it again with the next bucket.
