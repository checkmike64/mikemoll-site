TLDR

  • Financial freedom came from fixing pricing and process, not from working more.
  • Thirteen years of agency work almost burned Mike out before he rebuilt it.
  • Freedom follows a business you can repeat without running yourself down.

Mike joined Tyler Wagner on The Authors Unite Show to talk about his path to financial freedom. It was not a straight line, and he does not pretend it was.

Here is the honest version of how the shift happened.

The path started near burnout

Mike ran a Google Ads agency for thirteen years. From the outside it looked fine. Inside, it was heading toward burnout, and at one point he had to downsize the team. That low point forced a hard question. Was he building freedom, or just buying himself a stressful job? The honest answer pushed him to rebuild the whole thing from the ground up.

Pricing and process changed the math

The rebuild came down to a few unglamorous fixes. He had been undercharging, like most experts, so he learned to price for the problem instead of the hours. He simplified the offer down to one clear outcome and built a process he could repeat. He got picky about client fit. None of it was flashy. Together it changed the math of the business and gave him room to breathe.

Freedom is a business you can repeat

Mike's definition of financial freedom is not a big exit. It is a business that pays well and does not depend on him grinding. That comes from a simple offer, honest pricing, and clients who are a good fit. Do less, but do it well, and price it right. For more on building offers that hold, the DoGoodWork conversation is a good next listen, and the 21st Century Entrepreneurship episode covers the same ideas.
