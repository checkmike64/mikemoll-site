How to Time Block to Maximize Your Productivity

April 24, 202521 minInterview
Listen on Spotify Apple Podcasts Watch on YouTube

Tiffany Taylor coaches business owners on how to run their days from a calendar instead of a to-do list. Her short answer to the time question is plain. Give every hour a job, start with your vision, and let your calendar show you where the time goes. That's time blocking, and it beats a running list of tasks every week.

Tiffany is known in her space for being anti to-do list. In her view the to-do list is the weakest way to look at your time. When she reviews one, the work that grows a business tends to be missing. Sales and prospecting rarely make the cut. So she moves clients onto a digital calendar and away from the tools that pile up stress.

A lot of this comes down to how you define productivity. One person thinks it means getting more things done, so they chase a long list one item at a time. Another person thinks it means moving the needle with the least effort, so they pick fewer tasks with more weight. Tiffany starts every client with a written vision they can see. From there they set goals, and each goal runs through the SMART test: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound. She warns that up to 80% of a typical to-do list does nothing for your goals. You can knock out all of it and the needle still won't move.

Before you block anything, she suggests letting the calendar follow you for a week. Track what you already do, hour by hour. It forces you to face the reality of time, and most people don't love what they see. Then you color code. Work gets one color, rest another, exercise another. The surprise is what she puts in first. Sleep, rest, exercise, and family time go on the calendar before the work does. If you don't set your own office hours, nobody sets them for you. This is where the boundaries that keep burnout away get built.

She also knows the all-or-nothing crowd worries about slipping on day two. Her fix leans on the drag and drop in a tool like Google Calendar. Don't feel like sending emails this afternoon? Drag that block to tomorrow, then pull a task from another day into today. You still move real work forward, your time stays accounted for, and you keep the small win of skipping the thing you dreaded.

Tiffany and I also got into delegation. If you want the business to grow, you have to hire people who are better than you at the thing, which means humbling yourself first. My weak spot is copywriting. I know my story out loud, but putting it on a website is a different skill. Handing that off made the site stronger. We covered a close theme when I sat down with a guest on how to vet and hire the right service providers, and it ties into the wider point that a steady business runs on repeatable systems, not heroics.

On block length, she says there's no secret number. Watch your own focus and notice when it fades. She points to attention residue. After you start a task it takes 16 to 23 minutes to get fully absorbed. A 20-minute block ends about when you'd hit your stride. Get pulled away by a two-minute call and you still lose those 16 to 23 minutes of focus. Her starting point is a 45-minute block, then you adjust to your attention span. People with ADHD often need the opposite, sometimes four hours of deep focus before they lock in.

Her last big tip is theme days. One day for CEO work, one for marketing, one for content, one or two for calls. Fewer switches means more output. That maps to the maker versus manager idea. As a maker, I need a clear day for creative work. A single call in the afternoon pulls my head out of it for hours. The point she left me with is about guilt. The top complaint she hears about time is guilt, and she says it doesn't have to be that way. Sometimes changing the tool changes the way you talk to yourself.

Key takeaways

  • Drop the to-do list. It hides the work that grows a business, like sales and prospecting.
  • Start with a written vision you can see, then set SMART goals, then block the tasks.
  • Let the calendar follow you for a week before you plan. You need to see where time goes.
  • Put sleep, rest, exercise, and family time on the calendar first. Work fills in after.
  • Match block length to your attention span. Start at 45 minutes, then adjust.
  • Use theme days to cut task switching. One focus per day beats a scattered week.
About the guest

Tiffany Taylor

Tiffany Taylor is a productivity and mindset coach who helps accomplished business owners trade the to-do list for a calendar that fits their goals and their energy. She left a corporate operations role, traveled through dozens of countries, and now coaches high achievers on time management and focus. On this episode she shared her method start to finish: build a vision you can see, set SMART goals, then color-code the work into your calendar and match each block to your real attention span. She also offers a combined vision board and goal-setting workshop for anyone who wants the full walk-through.
