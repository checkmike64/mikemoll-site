## 2. THE SKILL WAS NEVER THE PROBLEM

You're good at what you do, and your clients know it. The gap is between the value your work creates and the number on your invoice. That number has been too low for a while now, and getting better at the craft doesn't close the distance on its own.

## 3. IS THIS YOU

You're earning between $7K and $20K a month. The business is there. But something's off in a way you feel before you can put words to it.

Here's what the pricing side looks like from the inside. You type the number into the proposal, sit with it for a second, start talking yourself down, and lower it before you send. You keep telling yourself you'll raise your rates once things feel stable. Things have felt unstable for two years.

The close looks like this. You finish a good call, the kind where everything clicks, and you hang up with no idea whether it's a yes. You couldn't tell someone what you did to make it land, so you can't do it again on purpose. And when the silence runs long enough, you'll sometimes drop the price yourself, before anyone's pushed back.

Then there's delivery. The scope drifts a little each week. They ask for one more thing, saying no feels harder than just doing it, and you hand over the unpaid extra again. You started this business to get some freedom, and somewhere along the way it turned into the opposite.

None of that means you're doing anything wrong. The offer is built wrong. That's a different problem, and a fixable one.

## 4. I'VE BEEN ON YOUR SIDE OF THIS

Corktown, 2017. A friend of mine was pacing his kitchen over whether he could raise a workshop from $1,700 to $2,200. Five hundred dollars had him tied in knots. So we sat down and worked out what a day in that room was worth to the company buying it. The answer was north of $100,000. He quoted $9,800. The client said yes the same day and never blinked.

I drove home a little rattled, because I'd been doing the same thing he was. Undercharging for years, in my own business, with the math sitting right there the whole time.

I've run service businesses since 2013. I built an agency to $700K a year and nearly went bankrupt twice, both times because I made everything more complicated than it needed to be. Since 2019 I've helped owners make this one shift. I've sold for Volvo and Patak's, run point as a fractional CMO for a company doing $20M a year, and built the sales process for a media company selling packages north of $100K. I've taken clients' rates up two to four times without losing the clients who mattered. That's why I can usually find your constraint fast. I've stood where you're standing, and I've seen the pattern enough times to know where to look.

## 5. THE COMPRESSION METHOD

This isn't a course. It's a diagnosis. You bring one heavy problem, and in a single conversation it narrows to the two or three moves that fix it. We work one constraint at a time, in order, because that's how you move a system. (The idea comes from Goldratt's Theory of Constraints.)

**[GRAPHIC: three-constraint chevron — Offer +30% price · Sales +30% close · Delivery −30% hours]**

Offer and positioning is what lets you raise the price without flinching. Sales and discovery structure is what turns a good call into a repeatable yes. Delivery with hard scope is what gives you your hours back.
